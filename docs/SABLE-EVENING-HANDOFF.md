# Sable opening: conversation and everyday behaviour

Implemented locally on the accepted shared TypeScript foundation. This pass changes the isolated Sable opening, not the original campaign or the mystery. The Inform experiment and the previous foundation milestone remain completed and preserved.

Launch **[the final build](http://localhost:52753/sable-trial.html)** (`sable-evening-4`). Its visible browser session is the separate final verification. The natural evening is preserved below on its own earlier build; it has not been relabelled as a clean final-build playthrough.

## Behaviour and reuse

- Shared handling distinguishes hesitation from competing interpretations. “Perhaps”, “maybe” and “I'm not sure” retain an identified offer without changing objects, time, observations or commitments. Diagnostics say `deferred`, separately from `clarified` and `handled`. Inspection and reload retain the offer; acceptance revalidates actual custody and contents.
- New orders and refills use the existing entity/custody machinery and the same validated `fillVessel` action. A new order selects an available declared vessel; an explicit refill uses the existing one. “Another coffee” clarifies new versus refill when location, contents or multiple vessels make the distinction consequential.
- Supply remains exactly the existing cup and glass. Unused vessels are available; an empty vessel on the accessible counter can be rinsed during service. A carried empty vessel is available for an explicit refill, not silently taken for a fresh order. Full, remote, inaccessible or destroyed vessels cannot be overwritten or teleported. No liquid or food simulation was added.
- Explicit predicates and negation take precedence over yes/no particles. Opinion replies, hesitation, explicit teasing, changed opinions and repetition have distinct authored responses. More specific declared topic names own their phrase: “closing party” does not also select the separate party-planning topic. Unsupported explicit follow-ups cannot silently borrow the previous topic.
- Sable's topics, detail responses and service wording live in authored declarations. Shared mechanics contain no Sable/Rowan/Kit parser branches. Presentation memory tracks prior replies, expressed opinions, served-drink jokes and declined offers; it is not authority for custody or evidence. The synthetic Rowan/Kit fixture exercises the same hesitation and acceptance code, alongside the existing second-vessel foundation tests. These characters remain non-canon.
- Ordinary company, thanks, declining and leaving work without opening the investigation. Coffee's introductory joke appears once per beverage/server, not on every refill. Showing evidence describes looking and handing it back. Unsupported interface requests are labelled separately from character dialogue.

## Context-priority routing (18 Sep, 282d42b)

- One anchored greeting rule, shared by `namedTopic` and the conversation opening; the drifted engine copy is removed.
- `namedTopic` takes the active subject: under hospital, photo, listing, investigation or report, a passing “night” or “evening” no longer redirects to plans, and an unplaced remark is answered with a clarification.
- Drink requests need a named drink, refill, fresh drink or “drink”; ordering words alone fall through, except that while Sable's offer is pending they accept it exactly as “yes” does.
- `companyReply` recognises questions about Sable's own preference and answers with the recorded position, offering, declining and arranging nothing.
- A look, visit or pop-in sentence that names another reachable room is travel; without a room, current behaviour is unchanged.

Sable suites 190 → 213 with no existing assertion changed; the [regression file](../tests/sable-context-routing.test.ts) replays the story-exploration submissions. Four browser routes, eight accessibility scans and legacy storage isolation passed on a fresh build ([browser results](../artifacts/sable-context-routing-20260918/browser-results.json)); `npm run check` passed with 1,320 tests. Story-exploration items #9, #22, #37, #47 and #48 are resolved; #29 now clarifies instead of joking, with its authored reply deferred to the expressive-speech task.

## Mechanical vocabulary layer (18 Sep, 9cf58e9)

- The standard parser vocabulary Inform gives for free, built generically in two new modules: [`vocabulary.ts`](../src/trial/vocabulary.ts) (verb table, synonym map, standard-verb hook, failure classifier) and [`scenery.ts`](../src/trial/scenery.ts) (data only: per-room scenery, the Sable and Vesper lines, per-room sense lines). `engine.ts` shrank by one line, `interactions.ts` is unchanged, nothing under `src/engine/` or `src/content/` moved. Conversation routing, `companyReply`, `requestDrink` and the greeting rule are untouched.
- Abbreviations (`x`, `l`, `i`, `z`); compass words answer with the room names the game uses; `exit`/`out`/`leave` at the bar name both outside destinations and stay put; bare `shop`, `booth`, `home`, `next door`, `through the curtain` and `back` travel; naming the current room is not a rejection; `sit`, `stand up`, `listen`, `smell`, `touch` give one in-fiction line and change nothing; every noun the room prose names is examinable; Sable and Vesper are examinable where present; `gin`, `ask for gin`, `a gin please`, `make it alcoholic` serve the alcoholic special as the menu promises. Bare `listen` keeps its silence meaning while Sable's decision is pending. `look behind`, `under` or `in` something is a glance: the examine line as one free line with nothing recorded, for scenery and real objects alike. `mug` names the cup.
- Standard verbs resolve before conversation and only for nouns that are not real objects (the same `entityCandidates` the object layer uses), and are gated on the word the player typed rather than the shared parser's near-miss-corrected verb, which turns “sip” into “sit”.
- Failed verb or object resolution now carries `{ stage, token }` on the result and in diagnostics: `unknown-verb`, `unknown-word`, `not-here`, `not-a-thing`, `partial`, `refused`. Message text is unchanged; the wording pass rewrites it per stage.
- Measurement: [the probe](../tests/sable-vocabulary-probe.test.ts) fires 1,577 natural inputs across eight states and writes [latest.tsv](../artifacts/vocabulary-probe/latest.tsv), tracked and byte-identical across runs on unchanged source. Handled rate before → after: bar opening 35% → 58%, bar after coffee 32% → 57%, bar holding evidence 32% → 57%, bar after show 31% → 50%, shop 17% → 29%, booth 8% → 18%, home 10% → 22%. Remaining rejections are dominated by `unknown-verb` (conversational sentences such as “a little”, “sounds fine to me”, “what do you have”) and, away from the bar, replies that Sable is not there to hear.
- Verification: [vocabulary suite](../tests/sable-vocabulary.test.ts) 144 tests; the seven existing Sable suites 213, no assertion changed; browser gate on fresh build `e8d3bf5` at port 52760 passed four paths, eight accessibility scans, reload/draft/bookmark and legacy storage isolation ([browser results](../artifacts/vocabulary-layer-20260918/browser-results.json)); `npm run check` passed with 1,465 tests in 22 files, 12 parser campaigns, 60 natural-language campaigns and 117 scenes.
- Forward rule for scenery (director): a new room gets one shared decorative line for background nouns; individual lines only for foregrounded items (people, exits, objects the prose invites the player to act on). The existing per-noun lines stand; do not extend them.
- Next, in this order: a short failure-wording pass (per-stage messages, OOPS, UNDO; `unknown-verb` should carry the word the player typed, not the near-miss-corrected verb; the token for ask/tell inputs should be the topic rather than “me”; and the classifier needs a `not-an-action` stage for input whose first word is not verb-shaped, currently reported as `unknown-verb` with tokens like “a”, “it's”, “what”, whose message belongs to the expressive-speech task, not the parser), then expressive speech and Sable's personal replies, and only then wink-nlp as a conservative fallback behind authored commands and declared typo correction. Out of scope throughout: engine migration, broad NLP rewrite, legacy-content deletion.

## Smoke-machine hook (18 Sep, 1975319)

- The opening's shop hook, an authored beat. Sable owns no smoke machine; it is Vesper's. After the first ordinary exchange of the first evening Sable asks, once, that the player tell Vesper it comes back after Thursday. A bare yes opens one agreement and Sable hears it; a bare no records nothing; a change of subject lets the question lapse; hesitation keeps it open at no cost. At the shop, naming Sable, the smoke machine or a message gets Vesper's one line, which shows the two as neighbours, closes the agreement if there is one and records Vesper's hearing; it names neither the photograph, the print, the lot's contents nor the past. Sable learns the message arrived once, by one of two exclusive paths: the player says so at the bar (thanks, recorded as heard from the player), or on a later evening Vesper has mentioned it (the head-round-the-door line on arrival, recorded as heard from Vesper). Goodnight with the message still to carry adds one line, once. Declined means silence.
- Two axes only. History is the observations ledger: Sable hears the acceptance (`favour-accepted`), Vesper hears the player's words (`smoke-machine-message`), Sable hears that it arrived (`message-delivered`, source naming the player or Vesper). The agreement is the new `agreements` ledger (`id`, `at`, `observer`, `words`, `status` open or kept, `keptAt`), the shape of `companyOffers` plus the close that the company offer never had. `validateTrial` rejects a duplicate, a future or an inconsistent agreement and a kept one without Vesper's hearing on record. Once-only lines use presentation memory. Save revision stays 3: the field defaults to empty, and a revision-three save without it loads with its pending favour intact. No scores.
- Where it lives: lines, trigger words and phrase lists are data in [`interaction-content.ts`](../src/trial/interaction-content.ts) (`favour`); the logic is the new [`agreements.ts`](../src/trial/agreements.ts); `engine.ts` gains the import, one call before `handleInteraction` (answers, Vesper's line, the thanks) and one after the room description (the offer, the goodnight line, the next-evening acknowledgement), plus `agreements` in the diagnostics field list; `question.kind` gains `favour`; one `contextualHelp` arm; `interactions.ts`, `vocabulary.ts`, `scenery.ts` and `src/engine` are byte-identical.
- Trigger: the offer follows the first handled `conversation:`/`service:` reply at the bar on day 0 that leaves the context absent or social/drink with no question open, no contradiction received and Vesper not already answered. It waits behind the drink offer and Sable's own complaint question, never follows the hospital account or the evidence, and never fires once the contradiction has been shown. The favour rides on the existing context as its question; any other exchange supersedes it.
- The bare-answer rule: the favour takes only whole-reply answers, two anchored lists in the data (the yes and no families the shared layer knows, the acceptances it does not, with "no problem" and "no worries" checked before negation), plus the shared hesitation forms and a qualified yes. The first draft applied `negated()` to any sentence and turned “Sable, don't order me a special with gin” into a decline; the routing suite rightly expects that negated instruction to stay rejected with no time passing. A sentence about anything else keeps its own handler and the favour stays open.
- Two existing assertions were rewritten, the only ones in three passes. Both answered the drink offer as the first ordinary exchange and then asserted `context.question` undefined; the favour is now the pending question there, so they say what they meant: the drink question is gone (`sable-foundations` “explicit complaint reply supersedes a later offer”: `question.subject` is not `drink`; `sable-playtest` “water please answers the pending choice”: `question.kind` is not `choose-drink`). Keeping them verbatim would have meant not offering after a drink exchange, against the approved trigger.
- Two refinements came from the probe rerun, after the implementation commit: the offer had fired after the contradiction was received (even after the decision), and a greeting or question addressed to the absent Sable at the shop (“hello sable”, “thanks sable”, “talk to sable”, “ask sable about the party”) had been taken as the message, closing the agreement. Both are guarded and tested. Still unchanged by design: “is that sable”, “that's sable” and “this looks like sable” at the shop are photo-identification questions and keep today's absent-Sable clarification.
- Measurement: the probe rerun is byte-identical across two runs. Per-state handled rate before → after: bar opening 58% → 58%, bar after coffee 57% → 58%, shop 29% → 30%, shop with evidence seen 29% → 30%, bar holding evidence 57% → 57%, bar after show 50% → 50%, booth 18% → 18%, home 22% → 22%. Eleven rows changed outcome: at the shop “tell vesper about sable”, “does vesper know sable”, “ask vesper about sable” and “do you know sable” (both shop states) now resolve to Vesper's line, intent `conversation:message`; after coffee, “yes”, “no” and “maybe” now answer the favour. 31 rows differ only by the appended offer line.
- Verification: [the hook suite](../tests/sable-hook.test.ts) 93 tests; the seven Sable suites 213 with the two rewrites above; vocabulary 144; `npm run check` passed with 1,558 tests in 23 files, build, narrative QA (117 scenes, no errors), scripted routes, 12 parser campaigns, 60 natural-language campaigns and the embodiment audit, and regenerated no tracked evidence. Browser gate on the fresh build `1975319` at port 52762: four direct/Vesper × formal/document paths, eight accessibility scans with no violations, reload/draft/bookmark and legacy storage isolation, no page errors ([browser results](../artifacts/smoke-machine-hook-20260918/browser-results.json)); the offer appears once in each route, after the tea, and is left to lapse.
- Next remains as ordered: the failure-wording pass, then expressive speech and Sable's personal replies, then wink-nlp as a conservative fallback.
