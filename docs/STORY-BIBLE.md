# Canon: The carbon copy

Velvet Quarter was a garment district before the flood of 2009. Velvet inhabits the old municipal night office. Its private members fund a mutual-aid ledger called the Umbrella; its management sells access while calling it discretion. The Static maintains an incomplete archive of displaced tenants. Neither institution owns the truth.

At 22:17 Friday, a guest-list printer issues a receipt from an obsolete overnight routing spool. At 22:19 Inez disconnects it. The invitation bears 23:41 because the print header is the delivery time, not the print time. This is never supernatural proof. The protagonist once signed for an envelope in their apartment building; their signature has been used as a neutral witness authorization. They did not knowingly consent to that role. Tonight an original ledger will change hands, and tenant names could become collateral.

Immutable: all central characters are over 25; the invitation is real; the timestamp is a delivery header; the tenant list contains vulnerable living people; protection can become paternalism; copies never establish innocence by themselves; NPCs only know facts they witnessed or received; nightlife operates independently of the player; leaving and refusing romance never prevent the core mystery.

Seed-variable truth:

- CARBON: Mara sent the invite through an old maintenance queue to obtain an independent witness. Inez suspects Celeste; Luca thinks Mara is selling him out. Carbon paper proves Mara's connection. The archive needs corroboration.
- PROXY: Celeste sent it to produce a witness for a transfer she expects to lose control of. Mara thinks Luca sent it. A brass authorization token authenticates Celeste. The club board has an advance copy.
- DEAD LETTER: Inez released an old queued invitation addressed to the previous tenant. She wants to identify who reused the authorization; Luca falsely thinks he commissioned it. A room register proves the intended recipient moved out. The witness named in the invitation is not the player.

Reading doesn't advance time. Player action can move scheduled consequences, never undo a fired event.

Relationship history: Mara and Luca formerly organized flood tenants; their split was about keeping names, not a failed romance. Celeste bought the building and preserved jobs while preserving her own leverage. Inez kept the night-office register and never told her employer. Mara believes loyalty requires asking. Celeste believes it requires anticipating. Luca thinks disclosure is a public good until his own source is at risk. Inez mistrusts records but retains every receipt.

Motifs: duplicate marks, umbrellas that belong to someone else, paper that darkens when warmed, disconnected machinery, names used with permission. Motel 27 is a transit address used by witnesses; whether there is one physical hotel remains unresolved. A key at the end opens the next chapter, not an unearned final answer.

## The hours between evidence and dawn

The document remains safe on the service table until the player decides its custody. After authentication, the player can choose two substantial companions. Each receives seven first-encounter beats and six closing beats, separated by another conversation and the document decision. Short departures remain available. No new district or extended Motel sequence is introduced.

Staffing events have named senders and explicit delivery paths. Mara chooses how to record Sera's emergency leave; Celeste resolves Noor's relief request; Luca decides how to disclose a repair gift and correct a false commitment; Inez handles Tom's absence without granting the player access to a staff channel. These events change interpretations and relationships, not the seeded invitation truth. A rare Mara/Inez overlap makes two staffing requests meet the same budget without exposing either worker's confidence.

Returning to Velvet shows closing work, late food, labelled keys, refunds and independent family plans. The player communicates the document outcome in the closing conversations before anyone comments on it. At home, selected ordinary memories return before reflection. Phone replies are authored choices, and ignored invitations do not freeze another person's evening.
