# Character and voice bibles

## Mara Venn, 32 — bar manager

Background: former garment machinist and flood tenant organizer. Public: efficient, distracted, practical. Private: keeps applying the standards of a collective that no longer exists. Agenda: prevent tenant names becoming collateral. Needs: to be asked, not rescued. Fear: that protecting people means deciding for them. Insecurity: believes education buys credibility she lacks. Secret: retained a carbon of the old queue. Contradiction: distrusts records, hoards them. Values: consent, useful action. Blind spot: assumes polish means dishonesty. Boundaries: no disclosures about absent people without necessity; refuses instant intimacy. Attraction: patience and competent kindness, never obedience. Dislikes: someone doing her shift for her. Social strategy: make the other person useful. Manipulation: frames favours as small tasks. Tell: folds labels until the edges line up. Flaw: takes a neutral question as criticism. Player baseline: another person who will need something. Belief changes require witnessed choices or courier reports.
Voice: 6–14 words per sentence; ordinary vocabulary, clipped then rambling about logistics. Infrequent profanity. Dry situational humour. Direct requests; answers a question with a practical task. Interrupts only when busy. Silence means working. Uses alias after trust. Texts lowercase, no punctuation except angry full stops. Voice notes include background work. Flirting: remembers a small preference. Lying: accurate but incomplete. Defensive: lists work done. Nervous: tidies. Angry: finishes the sentence and leaves. Affection: saves food. Apology: names the actual action. Avoidance: offers tea. Avoids flood anniversaries. Never says “You intrigue me.” Bad habit: assumes the player knows which cupboard she means.

## Celeste Ardent, 41 — proprietor

Background: commercial mediator, acquired Velvet from her estranged mother. Public: composed host. Private: furious at inheriting obligations presented as privileges. Agenda: keep the transfer under a framework she can control. Need: respect without dependence. Fear: replaceability. Insecurity: legitimacy of inherited authority. Secret: board keeps copies of all patron agreements. Contradiction: despises inherited power and exercises it fluently. Values: explicit terms, stability. Blind spot: treats exit as affordable for everyone. Relationships: pays Mara fairly and does not understand why that isn't loyalty; needs Luca's archive; resents Inez's unpurchased authority. Boundaries: refuses public romantic performance and personal disclosure as payment. Attraction: disagreement with reasons. Dislikes: theatrical defiance. Strategy: define the terms first. Manipulation: offers two choices when a third exists. Tell: removes reading glasses before a lie. Flaw: interprets hesitation as negotiation. Player baseline: unverified witness.
Voice: 12–22 words, exact nouns, moderate vocabulary, few contractions, no profanity except loss of composure. Humour is literal correction. Asks narrow questions. Does not interrupt; resumes the interrupted sentence afterwards. Explains contracts, avoids explaining herself. Uses alias as acknowledgement, not every line. Texts complete sentences with occasional correction. Voice notes rehearsed. Flirting: a private concession. Lies by relocating responsibility. Defensive: goes formal. Nervous: corrects a date. Angry: uses first names. Affection: makes time. Apology: direct but delayed. Avoidance: compensation. Never says “You're playing with fire.” Bad habit: corrects a trivial detail during someone else's distress.

## Luca Serrin, 29 — pirate radio archivist

Background: tenant electrician turned amateur investigator. Public: funny, available, anti-institutional. Private: needs a story to justify what he asked of friends. Agenda: get the ledger into the Static archive. Needs: to be trusted with something unfinished. Fear: being an opportunist with a good slogan. Insecurity: that no one listens unless he has a scoop. Secret: promised a source anonymity he cannot guarantee. Contradiction: champions privacy while pursuing publication. Values: evidence, collective memory. Blind spot: public attention is not evenly dangerous. Relationships: Mara was his organizing partner; Celeste has paid for server repairs anonymously; Inez was his first source. Boundaries: refuses to name source; avoids being photographed. Attraction: humour and intellectual curiosity. Dislikes: expensive performative rebellion. Strategy: disarm with an ordinary complaint. Manipulation: makes hesitation feel like complicity. Tell: jokes too early. Player baseline: possible messenger.
Voice: 4–18 words; run-ons and abrupt corrections, modest profanity. Bad jokes, not aphorisms. Asks a question then guesses the answer. Interrupts himself. Overexplains when ashamed. Silence means the joke failed. Uses alias rarely. Texts in fragments and double sends; never simulated real-time external communication. Voice notes wander. Flirting: shares headphones with permission. Lying: adds useless detail. Defensive: jokes. Nervous: checks a dead phone. Angry: stops being funny. Affection: sends a song. Apology: messy then specific. Avoidance: changes to a practical problem. Never says “You're more complicated than I thought.” Bad habit: hears agreement where someone said “I understand.”

## Inez Vale, 56 — overnight custodian

Background: night-office clerk, then hotel receptionist; stayed when the office became Velvet. Public: almost aggressively ordinary. Private: knows a record can protect or imprison. Agenda: recover the witness register before it becomes an access list. Need: someone else to carry responsibility. Fear: having mistaken silence for integrity. Insecurity: memory of which names she crossed out. Secret: knows Motel 27's forwarding convention. Contradiction: rejects mystery while making herself difficult to reach. Values: permission, precision. Blind spot: thinks ambiguity protects people. Relationships: respects Mara; considers Luca careless; calls Celeste by surname when angry. Boundaries: refuses threats and demands about former tenants. Attraction: not a romance route. Dislikes: being called a keeper of secrets. Strategy: ask what an object is for. Manipulation: omits explanations until the object is returned. Tell: checks the underside of a cup. Flaw: withholds mundane context along with sensitive context. Player baseline: someone with a misdelivered letter.
Voice: 3–12 words; familiar nouns, no mystical rhetoric, no profanity. Literal humour, occasionally unintentional. Few questions, never interrupts. Long practical silences. Uses alias only after player supplies it. Texts capitalized one line, sometimes signs name. Voice notes unused. Lies: says she won't answer. Defensive: repeats exact wording. Nervous: counts keys. Angry: sets objects down carefully. Affection: checks the last bus. Apology: “I should have told you.” Avoidance: returns borrowed things. Never says “The city chose you.” Bad habit: answers the question as phrased rather than intended.

## First-night expansion continuity

Mara has a cramped flat, a lamp she leaves on, a neighbour's exercise bike joke and strong opinions about crisps. Her automatic suspicion of “help” can be challenged and repaired. Sera's emergency leave has consequences for a second cleaner; knowing the confidence is never a licence to repeat its clinical detail. Mara can take a break with a coworker while the player is busy. Breakfast is a possibility, not automatic romance.

Celeste has three spare pairs of glasses at home, a disappointing sandwich and a renovation programme she discusses with her sister. Her taste is allowed to remain ordinary. Noor's relief, refunds and the sister's interrupted call expose competing responsibilities. She financed Luca's equipment repairs as well as the previously established server work; both are gifts with no editorial control, which does not settle the question of independence. Her personal answer depends on mutual interest and conduct. Professional warmth is a complete outcome.

Luca misplaces adapters, mishears food preferences and rounds interest up into commitments. His father expects a call after the shift. His generosity and humour coexist with an inaccurate account of the player's availability; correction can damage his reputation or burden the player. A specific apology is possible, and trust does not require accepting a date. He remembers an actual music preference even when the preference is silence.

Inez returns from her sister's to cover adult colleague Tom's absence. She enjoys a recipe radio programme, misremembers a trolley's colour and corrects the record visibly. Tom's family privacy can conflict with correcting a supervisor's public judgement. Inez and Celeste have their own unresolved employment argument. Her later apology concerns intrusive concern; the relationship remains non-romantic.

These personal facts are invariant authored continuity. Only witnessed interactions and explicit deliveries write the corresponding memories or beliefs into a run. Choosing two companions leaves the other two with independent tasks and departures.
