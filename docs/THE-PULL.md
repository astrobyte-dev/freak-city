# THE PULL and interpersonal intensity

THE PULL is a **private narrative-interest, attraction-context, chemistry, and thematic-engagement model**. It learns from the mature themes, aesthetics, character archetypes, relationship dynamics and social situations the player chooses to explore, avoid or leave unresolved.

The model observes explicit authored choices and optional player reflections. It supports authored scene relevance, adaptive description, chemistry, callbacks, character compatibility, mature-theme emphasis, relationship tension and replay variation. Refusal is not “secret interest”; uncertainty is not consent. Character boundaries and explicit user exclusions have priority over learned interest.

## Two complementary layers

Broad interests: curiosity, candour, privacy, defiance, connection and ritual. Each stores interest, familiarity, certainty, resistance, saturation, last display, last context and a hard exclusion bit. A single choice creates a tentative signal. It does not establish an identity.

The thematic layer is a registry of subcategories: negotiated authority, role reversal, ritual opt-out, symbolic belonging, private attention, performance observation, fashion materials, repair after embarrassment and check-ins after intense interactions. Each has:

- interest, familiarity, certainty, resistance and explicit uncertainty;
- intensity of thematic engagement (frequency band);
- contextual history, observation/participation orientation, trust requirement and private/either context;
- compatible characters, a hard exclusion, saturation and last display turn.

Physical/aesthetic attraction remains separate in `attractors`. The current chapter records a few style/personality combinations such as practical warmth and tailoring. It never infers personality or behavior from ethnicity or demographic membership.

`learnTheme` records explore, avoid or uncertain. Avoid decreases interest; uncertain records ambiguity and lowers certainty. Skip blocks learning. Disabling adaptation blocks both layers and attractor learning. Clearing resets the learned profiles while leaving explicit boundaries intact.

## Authored selection and pacing

Broad observations can appear only every third turn, with a five-turn per-cue cooldown and saturation decay. Mature thematic observations are rarer: every fourth turn, at least two consistent explorations, a six-turn cooldown, certainty threshold and compatible trust. Consecutive conflict/intimacy prevents an extra observation. Scene choices remain authored, with context, boundaries and NPC autonomy governing availability.

Selection uses curated observations and contrast variants. A familiar narrative theme can return as an ordinary logistical detail. Scene-location checks prevent club observations appearing in the apartment. Thematic compatibility prevents every NPC from providing the same experience. Context and orientation are kept in the profile to support future authoring; the current descriptions remain observational rather than generating actions for the player.

An authored example connects repeated exploration of negotiated authority with Celeste's trust to open a different introduction scene. It is optional; the core evidence and endings remain available without it. A profile cannot override the explicit power-exchange boundary.

The pacing director records the six latest categories: mundane, mystery, humour, conflict, intimacy, investigation, pressure and unease. It suppresses unnecessary adaptive additions after intense scenes. The larger pacing arc is authored rather than autonomously regenerated. It does not currently reorder whole scenes.

## HEAT and agency

HEAT represents a contextual mixture of attraction, chemistry, nervousness, embarrassment, vulnerability, anticipation and charged social tension. It is shown as a qualitative state. COMPOSURE tracks the protagonist's ability to formulate a response under that pressure.

At the upstairs objection, sufficient composure permits a direct challenge. Lower composure leaves options to request water, write an objection, negotiate or leave. High HEAT never forces romance, agreement or continued presence. The engine does not choose the player's behavior.

## Boundaries

Eleven user-facing categories have Allowed, Implied Only and Skip states. The taxonomy includes adult concepts for classification and compatibility; all current text is non-graphic.

- Allowed: the authored passage/choice can be considered, subject to all other conditions.
- Implied Only: the authored summary replaces the passage; fully themed choices are not offered.
- Skip: a safe passage or equivalent non-themed route is used; the theme is excluded from adaptation and themed choices. Skipping an optional scene costs no core evidence or ending.

Changing a boundary during an optional encounter replaces the entire scene with a neutral summary and an immediate exit. Boundary changes clear the displayed adaptive observation, remove learned entries for skipped themes, sanitize stored themed messages and mask previously recorded themed choice labels in the journal. Skipping surveillance removes the camera/REC decorative presentation. Effects and complete history remain inspectable in development; full erasure removes the local saves.

Theme lists and profile introspection are located in explicit settings, rather than presented as a fetish checklist during character creation. Reflection answers can remain uncertain. No explicit preference data is transmitted.
